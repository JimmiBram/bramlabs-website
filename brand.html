Brand

How BramLabs looks and sounds

One mark, one accent, three typefaces, and a plain voice. This page is the reference for anyone putting the studio’s name on something. Each rule comes with the reason for it, so you can decide the cases the rule does not cover.

The idea

Colour marks what a person decides

The studio’s argument is that building got cheap and good taste did not. The identity says the same thing. Everything built or structural is monochrome: black type, white boxes, a warm off-white page. The one colour, a warm yellow, is reserved for the part a person supplies. The liquid in the flask, the highlighted words in a headline, the numbers on the three pillars. If something is yellow, it is a judgement, not a mechanism.

Mark

The flask

A conical flask, half full of yellow. The glass is the studio: a shared way of working. The liquid is the product being worked on. There are two drawings of it, one for large sizes and one for small.

Full mark

For the hero, print, and anything larger than about 96 pixels. The white squares and the line inside the liquid are what the studio makes: products, taking shape. Keep them. They disappear at small sizes anyway, which is what the small mark is for.

Small mark

A single stroke outline with a flat yellow fill. Used in the header, the footer, and as the favicon, at anything from 16 to 96 pixels. It is drawn on a 24 unit grid with a 1.6 unit stroke, so the line stays the same weight as the text beside it.

  • Do
    Give it roomClear space on every side of at least the width of the flask’s neck. Nothing sits inside that space, including the wordmark, which gets its own gap.
  • Do
    Keep it uprightThe flask stands. It is never tilted, mirrored, or set at an angle to look dynamic. The liquid inside can move; the glass does not.
  • Don’t
    Recolour the liquidThe liquid is yellow, or ink when it sits on yellow. It is never a product colour, a gradient, or a photograph.
  • Don’t
    Add effectsNo drop shadow, glow, bevel, or outline. The site has none of these anywhere and the mark does not get an exception.

Wordmark

One word, two capitals

BramLabs

Schibsted Grotesk 700 · tracking -0.02em · mark at 1.1× the type size, gap 0.4×

BramLabs is written as one word with a capital B and a capital L, in running text as well as in the lockup. It is set in the display face at weight 700, always in ink or, on a dark surface, in paper. The small mark sits to its left, a touch larger than the type size, with a gap of about 0.4 times the type size.

The legal entity is Bram Labs ApS, two words. That spelling appears in the footer, on invoices, and in contracts. Nowhere else.

The domain is written bramlabs.io, lowercase, without a protocol.

Colour

Warm neutrals and one yellow

The page is paper, not white. Boxes on it are white. Type is a near-black with warmth in it, never pure black. The yellow is a fill; when yellow has to be read as text, it becomes amber.

  • Rule
    Yellow is earnedUse it for the thing a person decided: the highlighted words, the pillar numbers, the liquid. Not for decoration, not for a second call to action, not to make a section look lively.
  • Rule
    Never yellow text on paperYellow on the page fails contrast. Text that has to carry the accent uses amber. Yellow text only ever sits on ink.
  • Rule
    No gradients, no shadowsDepth comes from paper against white and from hairlines. Nothing on the site casts a shadow or fades from one colour to another.

Shape and motion

Soft boxes, still glass

Surface

Content sits in white boxes on the paper with a 1.25 rem corner radius. Buttons use a 6 pixel radius, so they read as controls rather than cards. A screenshot inside a box sits on the paper colour, so it reads as a well rather than a ledge. Sections are separated by space or a hairline, not by a change of background.

Motion

Three things move. Links change colour. A button fills with yellow from the bottom on hover, with a curved surface, like the flask filling. The liquid in the hero flask ripples on load and sloshes with the scroll. Nothing else animates, and all of it stops when the visitor asks for reduced motion.

Voice

Say the thing

The studio writes the way it works: short, declarative, and without a pitch. The reader is assumed to be intelligent and busy. Every sentence states something that could be wrong.

Do

  • Write short sentences. One idea each. Start a new one instead of adding a clause.
  • Say what we do, then what happens. “We release. What grows is shed off. What does not is closed down.”
  • Use British spelling. Organised, colour, programme.
  • Name the reason. A rule without a reason is a preference.
  • Let products speak for themselves on their own sites, in their own voice.

Don’t

  • No exclamation marks. Nothing on the site is that exciting.
  • No superlatives about ourselves. Revolutionary, world-class, cutting-edge, and their cousins.
  • No jargon that describes us instead of the work. Leverage, seamless, ecosystem, journey, empower.
  • No em dashes. Use a full stop, a comma, or a new sentence.
  • No names on the door. The studio speaks as “we”; the people behind each product are on that product’s site.
  • Instead of“We leverage cutting-edge AI to supercharge product velocity!”
  • Write“We build with AI. Everyone is fast now. The advantage is knowing what to make.”
  • Instead of“Ready to transform your business?”
  • Write“If one of our products interests you, write.”
