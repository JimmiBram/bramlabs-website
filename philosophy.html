Philosophy

What is left when AI can build everything

The argument BramLabs is built on, in six parts. It is short because it is simple, and simple because we have tested it on real products.

01

Building is no longer the constraint

Every software organisation we have worked in was shaped by one scarcity: the number of hours it takes to write, test, and ship code. Headcount, planning, budgets, the length of a roadmap, the size of a bet. All of it was downstream of that number.

That number has collapsed. A model with a terminal and a good brief writes most of a product, tests it, wires it to the services it needs, reads its own logs, and ships. It does this in the time it used to take to schedule the meeting about it. The cost of a feature is approaching the cost of describing it precisely.

We do not treat this as a prediction. The products in this studio were built this way, by people whose job is the product rather than the typing.

02

The scarce input moved

When a constraint disappears, the next one becomes visible. If building is close to free, a product is decided by two things: what you ask for, and what you accept.

What you ask for depends on knowing the domain. Which problem matters, who has it, what they already tried, what the regulator will say, what the invoice looks like. What you accept depends on taste: whether this screen is right, whether this feature belongs, whether the product is done or merely finished.

The model has no opinion about whether the product should exist. Someone has to.

That someone is the whole studio. Everything else can be generated.

03

Three things a model cannot supply

Domain knowledge. A model knows what is written down. Most of what makes a product right in a given field is not written down. It is in how the work is actually done, which is learned by doing it. We build where someone in the house has done that work, and when the domain is not ours, we build with a good friend of the house who owns it.

Taste. A model will add whatever you ask for, and it will suggest more. Taste is the ability to look at the forty things that could go in and know which three belong, and to say no to the rest with a reason. It is also knowing when something is good enough to ship and when it is not, which no test suite can tell you.

Experience. Launching is a day. Running is years. Pricing that survives contact with a procurement department, onboarding that works for the second customer as well as the first, support, security, backups, the page at 03:00. Experience is knowing what those cost before you pay for them, and designing so you pay less.

04

The dimensions of a good product

A product is good along several dimensions at once, and AI can execute on every one of them. It cannot decide any of them. This is the list we hold each product against.

  • a
    The problemReal, frequent, and expensive enough that someone will pay to make it go away.
  • b
    The scopeWhat the product is not. The edges are where most products get lost.
  • c
    The detailsThe hundred small choices a user feels but cannot name. Latency, defaults, wording, what happens on the empty state.
  • d
    The economicsWhat it costs to serve one customer, what they pay, and whether the gap survives growth.
  • e
    The operationReliability, support, security, and the plan for the day it breaks.
  • f
    The trustWhether a customer believes it will still be here next year, and whether they are right.

A studio built around good taste is a studio that can hold all six in view at once, for every product, without a committee.

05

What this does to the shape of a studio

If the scarce input is good taste, the studio should be organised around the people who have it, and it should keep them as close to the product as possible. So we run small. One builder carries a product from idea to paying users, with AI as the workshop. We add a second person when the product has earned it, not when a plan says so.

We build the whole system first, thin, so the hard problems appear in the first week rather than the sixth month. We put the product in front of real users as early as it can survive, and we let what they do set the roadmap. And we treat each product as its own company, with its own users and its own numbers, so the call about whether to keep going is made honestly.

06

What we do not believe

That AI is the product. It is the way we build, and often a part of what we build. A product is described by the problem it solves. If the only thing to say about it is that it uses AI, it is not ready.

That speed is the advantage. Everyone is fast now. Speed without good taste produces more wrong things sooner. The advantage is knowing what to make, and being able to make it before the knowledge goes stale.

That a bigger team is a safer team. Every person added to a product adds coordination, and coordination is where taste gets averaged out. The safest team is the smallest one that holds the whole product in its head.

That this is finished. The tools change monthly, and so does the line between what a model does and what a person decides. We revise this page when the products teach us something. The date at the bottom is real.
